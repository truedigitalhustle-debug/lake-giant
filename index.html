The method

Three clips. One giant. One reference for each thing.

Each clip is 10 seconds with six or seven cuts written into the prompt, so the model shoots it like an edit. The hero, the giant and the monster each get their own reference picture, which is what keeps them the same from clip to clip.

Make a character sheet of yourself.Front, side and back, in the outfit you want on screen. Mine is a black tank top, cargo pants, boots and gloves. This is @image1 in every clip you appear in.
Design the giant as a still image first.Run the giant prompt below in an image model until you get one you love. Keep people out of this picture. This is @image2 .
Generate Clip 1, the invasion.Seedance 2.5, 16:9, 720p, 10 seconds, audio on, text only. You are not in this clip, so it needs no references.
Screenshot the monster from Clip 1.Grab the clearest frame of the alien commander. This is @image3 in Clip 3, so the monster that gets punched is the same one that attacked the city.
Generate Clip 2 and Clip 3.Load the references in order so the tags line up: your sheet, then the giant, then the monster frame for Clip 3.
Fix five seconds, not ten.When one half of a clip is wrong, cut the prompt down to just those shots, generate a 5-second clip and splice it in.
Stitch, score, and add your CTA.Drop the clips in order, lay one music track across all of it, and put your call to action on the final look into the camera.

What I learned the hard way

Five things that saved this video

If it reminds you of a movie, scrap it. My first idea was a suit of armor, and the first still came back looking like a superhero everyone already knows. I threw it out and asked what only my city has. Chicago has a lake.
Keep people out of your reference stills. My first giant still had a small man in a jacket standing in front of it. The AI copied that jacket onto me in every shot from behind, even though my sheet shows a tank top. I cropped him out and the jacket was gone.
Give the monster a reference too. From text alone, the monster changed design between Clip 1 and Clip 3. One screenshot of it as a reference made them match.
Say how big things are, in buildings and people. My first punch shot had the monster barely taller than me. "As tall as twenty humans stacked head to toe, its head level with the tenth floor" reads better than "huge."
Test the same prompt on two models. I ran Clip 1 on Kling 4.0 and Seedance 2.5. Kling hit the cut timing more exactly. Seedance looked more like a real film, with a real Chicago skyline, so Seedance won for this one.

Don't re-run the whole clip

Both of my fixes were 5-second clips: the back half of Clip 2 for the jacket, and the front half of Clip 3 for the monster. Cut the prompt down to the shots you need, keep the same references, and splice the new half over the old one.
